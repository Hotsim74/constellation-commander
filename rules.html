Constellation Commander Rulebook

Constellation Commander

The rulebook, one board, version 1.15. Every rule here is what the game does right now. Nothing is final.

The game in four lines. You are trying to claim more of the sky than the bot. Line up stars: anything over 3 in a row gets you a drone. A drone is like a checkers or chess piece you move around to claim space and fight. Only scouts claim stars for you; 69 squares wins, or a siege drone standing next to the bot's Command drone.

The board

One 11 × 11 board. Every square holds a star of one of five colors, or an asteroid. Under the stars is a red-and-white checkerboard. The middle row is the seam, the line between your half and the bot's. Stars fall toward whoever is moving. On your turn every column drops toward your back row and new stars come in from the top edge; on the bot's turn everything rises toward its back row and new stars come in from the bottom. Asteroids split a column, so nothing falls through one; clear it with a missile and the column opens.

There are no walls. You start owning a block of nine squares, three by three, at the middle of your back row, with your Command drone, a scout, a siege drone and a shield inside it. Each side starts with asteroids in its own half; the number comes from the ratings.

Asteroids

Asteroids can't be swapped, matched, or entered, and they split a column so nothing falls through them. A missile clears one. They drift: clear 8 or more stars on your side in one turn, cascades counted, and every asteroid on the board moves one square toward you, the way the stars are falling. An asteroid that runs into a lone drone destroys it and takes its square. Into a stack, it knocks one power off and jumps over to the next square; a drone on that square is destroyed. Two squares is the most an asteroid ever moves in a turn. A wall, the Commander, the edge of the board or another asteroid stops it.

The fog

Off by default: the whole board shows. Settings has Fog of war with two ways to play in the dark. From the seam: you see your own half, and beyond it only one square around each of your drones (shields see nothing) and squares a scout has claimed, which stay lit all game. Everything dark: even your own half starts dark. In the dark you can't swap, move through, or shoot at a square you can't see, shields may only stand on squares you own or have cleared, and the bot lives under the same fog.

Start and turns

You begin with a block of nine squares at the middle of your back row: your Command drone in the back middle, a scout on its left, a siege drone on its right, a shield at the front edge, already stacked to power 2, and one missile, which your scout loads at once. You move first.

Against the bot the game is relaxed unless you turn Timers on in Settings: no move timer, no clock. With Timers on it is a 20-minute chess clock and 60 seconds a move.

A turn: missiles first, if you want. Then one action: swap two stars side by side to make a line, move one drone, claim with a scout, warp, fire a siege drone, or ram. A swap that makes no line snaps back for free.

Your land is yours. Neither star of a swap may sit on a square the other side owns; you swap on open land or your own. Every square a scout claims is one the other side can no longer play on. A line that forms entirely on their land by itself, after a cascade, clears but pays nothing.

Shapes

Lines count across, down, and on the diagonal, where they need 4 or more. A 3 clears stars and pays nothing. A shape pays only if part of it is on your side of the seam, and the drone appears on that part, in the color of the star that made it. After a match the stars flow, and any lines that form on their own are yours too.

You can hold 9 drones; turrets do not count. At 9, a shape pays what the right column says, or stacks into your own drone if it was made under one.

The drones

Every drone wears its letter: M command, S scout, G shield, R raider, H hunter, W warp, X siege, B bomb. Yours are red letters on a gold rim, the bot's white on blue. The box at a drone's bottom-left is the star color it rides for free; a stack shows one box per color, a hunter's box is every color, a raider's is the red or white of its squares, and shields and sieges have none.

Who takes whom. Raiders take any drone they land on, except a siege. Hunters fight with missiles only. Shields take scouts, hunters, warps and shields by moving next to them on your land. Sieges take nothing and cannot be taken; four enemy drones around one destroy it. Nothing takes the Command drone by landing. Every take earns a missile for your Command drone. Tap any drone, yours or the other side's, to see where it can go and what it can take. After every move the game says what happened and why; turn that off in Settings under Explain moves.

  • Scout (S). Moves 1 square any way. Fires one missile a turn. Claim: the 3 × 3 around it, open land becomes yours, enemy land becomes disputed, disputed becomes yours, and the 3 × 3 is cleared of fog for good. Two claims per scout; the second uses it up.
  • Shield (G). Moves 1 square, only onto squares you own or have cleared. It sees nothing in the fog and takes nothing there. It takes a scout, hunter, warp or shield by moving next to it on your land. Standing still it takes nothing. Standing next to an enemy siege drone it blocks the shot.
  • Raider (R), the old strike. Red or white, shown in the box on its disc: the color of the squares it was born on and the only squares it can ever stand on, since a diagonal slider never changes square color. Every square shows its own red or white in its top-right corner. Slides the full diagonal in any direction until it hits something or the board's edge, and takes any enemy drone it stops on, except a siege. It may slide into the dark. Where it stops, the fog lifts one square around it while it stands there. Once made, the slide is made.
  • Hunter (H). Moves 1 square plus up to 2 free steps on any star color, bending as it goes: three squares of reach. Fires two missiles a turn: both are aimed first, then both hit at once and the stars drop once. It loads two from the Command drone by standing beside it. It cannot take drones by landing; it fights with missiles.
  • Warp (W). Moves 1 square. Or, as your action, jumps to any cleared square on your side that isn't on or next to an enemy shield. On landing, every unclaimed square in the 3 × 3 becomes yours, enemy land stays enemy, and it takes enemy drones only on squares that are now yours. That uses it up.
  • Command drone (M). Your Commander, as a piece. Moves 1 square, and rides the star colors of the drones stacked into it. It can hold two more drones (power 3). Nothing takes it by landing or by missile; missiles and bombs only strip drones off its stack. Only a siege drone ends it: a siege standing next to it, with no shield beside the siege or the Command drone, wins the game at once. An asteroid that drifts into it jumps over it (or knocks one drone off its stack); an asteroid that jumps another drone and lands on it destroys it, and the other side wins. Keep it home, or keep a shield beside it.
  • Siege (X). Moves exactly 1 square a turn and is the one drone that walks into the dark freely. It can't take drones and can't be taken by them. Walk it next to the enemy Command drone with no enemy shield beside either and you win. Four enemy drones around it destroy it. If a shield is in the way it may ram that shield, and both are gone. Two per side at once.
  • Bomb (B). Moves 1 square, or rides its own star color for free. Goes off at the end of your third turn after you get it. The blast runs up, down, left and right from where it sits, along each arm as far as you have cleared the fog, and takes every asteroid, star and drone in its path, yours included. The Command drone stands, though a blast strips its stack. The other player sees how far it reached.

Moving and stacks

Free rides. Every step onto a star of a drone's own color is free, so it can ride a lane of its color as far as it goes and spend its one paid step before or after. Shields and sieges get no rides. Strikes slide instead.

Stacks. Move one of your drones onto another of yours to stack them. The stack keeps the kind you landed on, adds power up to 3, and gains the other drone's star color so it rides more lanes. The game asks before it stacks. Power is armor against missiles: a power 3 stack takes three hits. It's no help in a fight: a raider takes the whole stack in one landing.

Missiles

Missiles are stock on your Command drone. Every take earns one there, a drone destroyed by a missile included. Only scouts and hunters fire, and they have to load first: a scout or hunter standing beside your Command drone takes missiles from it, one for a scout, two for a hunter, when it ends a move there and again at the start of each of your turns while it stays. A drone carries its missiles with it; the orange bubble on its disc is its count, and the Command drone's bubble is the stock. Then, before your action, a scout fires once a turn and a hunter twice. A missile flies 3 squares from the firing drone, any direction (5 squares when the fog of war is on), at anything you can see. At a drone it takes one power. At an asteroid or star it destroys it, the stars flow, and any lines that form are yours. Nothing is sacrificed to fire.

Salvage (a Settings option, off by default). With it on, a destroyed drone does not pay its missile at once. It leaves a wreck on its square, glowing, and the missile stays in the wreck until a drone, yours or the other side's, ends its move on that square. Taking a drone by landing on it puts you on the wreck, so those still pay at once; a missile kill, a shield's sweep, a bomb or a surrounded siege leave a wreck somebody has to walk to. Wrecks fade after five rounds. An asteroid that crushes a drone leaves nothing. The stars under a wreck keep swapping and falling as usual. In a two-phone match the host's setting counts.

Turrets

When your Command drone holds 10 missiles, tap it and choose Build a turret, then an empty square you own. The ten missiles become a turret, letter T, with eight barrels. It never moves. At the start of each of your turns it fires once, by itself, at an enemy drone in the 8 squares beside it, a siege first if one is there, otherwise the weakest. Each shot takes one power, and a drone it destroys pays a missile to your stock like any kill. A turret is a drone for every other purpose: raiders take it by landing, shields sweep it, missiles and bombs destroy it, an asteroid rolls over it, but it does not count toward your 9 drones. The bot builds them too, from skill 40.

Winning

Your starting squares count as yours. Claims and warps take more. Hold 69 squares of the 121 (a setting, 60 to 100) and you win, or stand a siege drone next to the enemy Command drone with no shield beside, or let an asteroid land on it. With Timers on, running out of clock loses.

Your first game

The first time you open the game it offers to walk you through: a dark board, no clock, and the game lights the square to tap and waits. Move the scout, claim and watch the fog lift, make a match and watch the drone appear. It's in Settings any time as Guided first game.

After that, a quieter hand: with the bot at skill 50 or under, if you sit 30 seconds on your turn the two stars of the best swap light up green.

Playing a friend

Settings has Play a friend. One phone taps Start a match and gets a 4-letter code; the other either types the code or finds the match under Open matches and taps it. The phone that started runs the game, and both phones show the same board, each with its own side at the bottom. Your name is the one in Settings, and your rating travels with you: the asteroids are set from both ratings the way they are against the bot, and the game moves your rating at full weight. Leaving an unfinished match counts as a loss. If a phone drops off, open the game again and it picks the match back up.

The bot's skill

Bot skill moves by tens, 0 to 100. Two things change with it. At skill S the bot plays its best move S% of the time and a random legal move otherwise, so skill 10 is mostly noise and skill 100 is never random. And what its best move knows grows a step every ten; each level keeps everything below it.

  • 0. Random. It swaps and moves at random and never fires.
  • 10. It knows which lines pay a drone and claims land with its scouts.
  • 20. It fires missiles at your drones and takes what it can reach.
  • 30. It keeps its Command drone home and sweeps with its shields on its own land.
  • 40. It stacks drones for armor and clears asteroids on its side.
  • 50. Its hunters fire both missiles, its sieges walk toward your Command drone, and it warps for land.
  • 60. It will not park a drone where your raiders, hunters or shields can take it next turn.
  • 70. It picks swaps for the cascade too: more stars cleared, and the asteroid drift when it can reach eight.
  • 80. It goes after wrecks when Salvage is on and walks its bombs toward your drones.
  • 90. It keeps a shield beside its Command drone at all times.
  • 100. Every turn is its best move. Nothing is random.

Ratings and handicaps

You start at 1000 and the number moves the chess way after each game. The bot at skill S is rated 800 + 12 × S: skill 10 is 920, the beginner setting and the default; 50 is 1400; 100 is 2000. A game against the bot moves your rating in proportion to its skill, a tenth at skill 10, half at 50, the full amount at 100. Starting a new game while one is under way counts as a loss.

With Handicap: asteroids from ratings on, the asteroid counts are set from the gap: one asteroid per 50 points, half added to the stronger side's half, half taken off the weaker side's, from the base in the Your asteroids box. The rating change is then worked out from the gap that is left after the rocks.
