The board

One 11 × 11 board. Every square holds a star of one of five colors, or a rock. Under the stars is a red-and-white checkerboard. The middle row is the seam: stars are born there and drift toward each back row. Clear stars on your side and the ones nearer the seam slide down to fill in, with new stars born at the seam.

Your Commander sits in the middle of your back row inside a wall of five squares, and the five squares in front of the wall are your yard. You own those from the start. Rocks can't be swapped, matched, or entered. Each side starts with rocks in its own half; the number comes from the ratings.

The fog

Everything starts dark except your own land, one square around each of your drones (shields see nothing), and squares a scout has claimed, which stay lit all game. You can't swap, move through, or shoot at a square you can't see. The bot lives under the same fog.

Start and turns

You begin with your Commander, walls and yard, two shields at the ends of the yard, one scout on your back row, and one missile. You move first.

Against the bot the game is relaxed unless you turn Timers on in Settings: no move timer, no clock. With Timers on it is a 20-minute chess clock and 60 seconds a move.

A turn: missiles first, if you want. Then one action: swap two stars side by side to make a line, move one drone, claim with a scout, warp, fire a siege drone, or ram. A swap that makes no line snaps back for free.

Shapes

Lines count across, down, and on the diagonal, where they need 4 or more. A 3 clears stars and pays nothing. A shape pays only if part of it is on your side of the seam, and the drone appears on that part, in the color of the star that made it. After a match the stars flow, and any lines that form on their own are yours too.

You can hold 10 drones. At 10, a shape pays what the right column says, or stacks into your own drone if it was made under one.

The drones

Every drone wears its letter. Yours have a gold rim, the bot's a blue one.

  • Scout (C). Moves 1 square any way. Fires one missile a turn. Claim: the 3 × 3 around it, open land becomes yours, enemy land becomes disputed, disputed becomes yours, and the 3 × 3 is cleared of fog for good. Two claims per scout; the second uses it up.
  • Shield (G). Moves 1 square, only onto squares you own or have cleared. It sees nothing in the fog and takes nothing there. It takes a scout, hunter, warp or shield by moving next to it on your land. Standing still it takes nothing. Standing next to an enemy siege drone it blocks the shot.
  • Strike (S). Red or white, shown on the disc, for a rule still to come. Slides the full diagonal in any direction until it hits something or the board's edge, and takes any enemy drone it stops on, except a siege. It may slide into the dark. Where it stops, the fog lifts one square around it while it stands there. Once made, the slide is made.
  • Hunter (H). Moves 1 square plus up to 4 free steps on any star color, bending as it goes. Fires two missiles a turn: both are aimed first, then both hit at once and the stars drop once. Takes any enemy drone it lands on except a siege.
  • Warp (W). Moves 1 square. Or, as your action, jumps to any cleared square on your side that isn't on or next to an enemy shield. On landing, every unclaimed square in the 3 × 3 becomes yours, enemy land stays enemy, and it takes enemy drones only on squares that are now yours. That uses it up.
  • Siege (X). Moves exactly 1 square a turn and is the one drone that walks into the dark freely. Beside the enemy wall, Fire clears the walls next to it; beside the Commander, Fire takes it and wins. It can't take drones and can't be taken by them. Four enemy drones around it destroy it. A shield beside it blocks the shot; it may ram that shield instead, and both are gone. Two per side at once.
  • Bomb (B). Moves 1 square, or rides its own star color for free. Goes off at the end of your third turn after you get it. The blast runs up, down, left and right from where it sits, along each arm as far as you have cleared the fog, and takes every rock, star and drone in its path, yours included. Commander and walls stand. The other player sees how far it reached.

Moving and stacks

Free rides. Every step onto a star of a drone's own color is free, so it can ride a lane of its color as far as it goes and spend its one paid step before or after. Shields and sieges get no rides. Strikes slide instead.

Stacks. Move one of your drones onto another of yours to stack them. The stack keeps the kind you landed on, adds power up to 3, and gains the other drone's star color so it rides more lanes. The game asks before it stacks. Power is armor against missiles: a power 3 stack takes three hits. It's no help in a fight: a strike takes the whole stack in one landing.

Missiles

Every take earns one. Only scouts and hunters fire, before your action, a scout once a turn, a hunter twice. A missile flies 5 squares from the firing drone, any direction, at anything you can see. At a drone it takes one power. At a rock or star it destroys it, the stars flow, and any lines that form are yours. Nothing is sacrificed to fire.

Winning

Your yard, walls and Commander count as yours. Claims and warps take more. Hold 46 squares and you win, or take the enemy Commander with a siege drone. With Timers on, running out of clock loses.

Your first game

The first time you open the game it offers to walk you through: a dark board, no clock, and the game lights the square to tap and waits. Move the scout, claim and watch the fog lift, make a match and watch the drone appear. It's in Settings any time as Guided first game.

Ratings and handicaps

You start at 1000 and the number moves the chess way after each game. The bot at skill S is rated 800 + 12 × S: skill 10 is 920, the beginner setting and the default; 50 is 1400; 100 is 2000. A game against the bot moves your rating in proportion to its skill, a tenth at skill 10, half at 50, the full amount at 100. Starting a new game while one is under way counts as a loss.

With Handicap: rocks from ratings on, the rock counts are set from the gap: one rock per 50 points, half added to the stronger side's half, half taken off the weaker side's, from the base in the Your rocks box. The rating change is then worked out from the gap that is left after the rocks.
